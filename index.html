Kiernan Jennings

PhD Candidate, Chemical & Biological Engineering
University of Wisconsin–Madison

I develop optimization models and decomposition algorithms for large-scale problems in energy systems. My current work focuses on grid-integrated electrolysis: a multi-scale framework that links electrolyzer operation in electricity markets to long-term equipment degradation and replacement, and a Benders decomposition algorithm that makes the resulting models solvable at scale.

I am a third-year PhD candidate, minoring in Industrial and Systems Engineering, co-advised by Victor M. Zavala and Styliani Avraamidou. I received my BS in Chemical Engineering from the University of Connecticut.

Research Curriculum Vitae

Focus Areas

Large-Scale Optimization

Multi-scale mixed-integer programs with millions of binary variables, built to link short-term operation with long-term planning decisions.

Decomposition Algorithms

Benders and graph-based decomposition for models too large to solve monolithically, including models that embed machine learning surrogates.

Energy Systems

Operation and planning of electrolyzers and other electrochemical systems participating in electricity markets.
