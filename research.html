Research

Models and algorithms for large-scale optimization, with applications in energy systems.

Overview

Decisions in energy systems — where to site generation, how to schedule resources, how to plan for uncertainty — are naturally posed as optimization problems. At realistic scale, these problems become difficult to solve: they couple many time periods and spatial regions, embed nonlinear physical models, and must be re-solved many times under different scenarios.

My work develops methods that make these problems tractable without discarding the structure that makes them meaningful. Two directions run through it: exploiting decomposable structure through distributed algorithms, and replacing expensive model components with learned surrogates that preserve solution quality.

Directions

Distributed & Decomposition Methods

Graph-based formulations and ADMM-style decomposition for problems whose structure can be partitioned across subsystems, enabling parallel solution of models too large for a single solve.

Surrogate-Based Optimization

Learned approximations of costly subproblems and physical models, embedded directly in optimization formulations, with attention to when surrogate error changes the resulting decision.

Explainability in Optimization

Methods for interpreting and communicating why a large-scale optimization model produces the solution it does — which constraints bind, which inputs drive the outcome, and how robust the result is.

Energy Equity & Sustainability

Formulating reliability, emissions, and distributional-equity objectives as explicit terms in energy planning problems, and examining the trade-offs among them.
