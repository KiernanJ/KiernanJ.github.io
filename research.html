Research

Large-scale optimization and decomposition algorithms, with applications in energy systems.

Overview

My research develops optimization models and decomposition algorithms for energy systems whose decisions span very different time scales. The central application is grid-integrated electrolysis: deciding how electrolyzers should operate in electricity markets hour by hour while accounting for how that operation degrades the equipment over decades. Coupling these scales produces mixed-integer programs with millions of binary variables, so much of my work is on decomposition methods that make them solvable.

Directions

Multi-Scale Optimization for Grid-Integrated Electrolysis

A mixed-integer linear programming framework that couples demand-response operation of electrolyzers in day-ahead and real-time markets with usage-based degradation and stack replacement scheduling, so short-term operating decisions are priced against their long-term cost.

Aggregate-Informed Benders Decomposition

A Benders decomposition algorithm tailored to the multi-scale structure of these models. On 40-year instances with up to 5.6 million binary variables, where monolithic solves run out of memory, it reduces final optimality gaps from 85% to under 5%.

Decomposing Surrogate-Embedded Models

PlasmoSurrogates.jl, a Julia framework built on Plasmo.jl for decomposing optimization models that combine machine learning surrogates with algebraic models. Related work with collaborators on decision-focused surrogate modeling for mixed-integer nonlinear programs.

Electrochemical Systems in Electricity Markets

Market participation of electrochemical devices beyond electrolysis, including multi-market bidding and demand response for redox reservoir-based devices using two-stage stochastic optimization, and carbon-aware operation of grid-integrated electrolysis.
